# Global History, Culture & Changemakers — V1.25

V1.25 adds a permanent, age-adaptive **Global History, Culture & Changemakers** district to Orish's World.

## Product rule
Black history is not a seasonal add-on. The child can explore Black inventors, scientists, engineers, health leaders, journalists, athletes and community organisers throughout the year, alongside respectful learning about cultures and histories around the world.

## Starter changemakers
The local prototype includes source-backed starter journeys for Alice Augusta Ball, Frederick McKinley Jones, Norbert Rillieux, George R. Carruthers, Kofoworola Abeni Pratt, Olive Morris, Claudia Jones and Arthur Wharton.

The intent is not to make these eight people a definitive canon. They prove the content architecture for adding many more people from Africa, the Caribbean, Britain, the Americas and the wider world, including people usually omitted from children's history products.

## Culture Explorer starter packs
The prototype currently demonstrates four culturally distinct learning packs:
- Kente weaving — Ghana; Asante and Ewe contexts.
- Whakapapa and ancestry — Māori / Aotearoa New Zealand.
- Día de los Muertos — Indigenous festivity dedicated to the dead in Mexico, with contemporary variation acknowledged.
- Ukiyo-e woodblock prints — Edo-period Japan.

Each pack must identify a place/community/time context and include a respect note. The engine must not turn a living culture into a costume, stereotype or generic decorative style.

## Evidence architecture
Every approved profile contains a `sourceTrail` with source organisation, source title and URL. Child mode displays the source organisation and title but does not open the live web. This keeps the prototype compatible with the existing no-open-web child safety boundary.

Historical claims should be classified during editorial review as:
- well-supported fact;
- interpretation/context;
- disputed or uncertain claim;
- not approved for child content.

Do not convert a popular social-media claim into a child fact card merely because it is widely repeated.

## Age adaptation
- 0–2: parent-led looking/listening and simple true stories; no independent history quiz.
- 2–4: short people/place/culture stories with movement, matching and making.
- 4–6: problem → idea → change story trails.
- 7–9: cause/effect, clues and source-aware history detective missions.
- 10–12: context, barriers, evidence and source organisations.
- 13–16: historiography, power, colonial context, missing voices, contested credit, source reliability and uncertainty.

## Privacy
Culture activities must never require a child to disclose ethnicity, religion, immigration status, family history or ancestry. Personal family stories may be used only if a parent deliberately opts into a future private family-history feature with separate privacy design.

## Editorial review before launch
Starter content is a prototype curriculum pack. Before public child use it requires education/editorial review for accuracy, age suitability, cultural sensitivity, language and source currency. New history content should use primary sources or reputable archives, museums, universities, scientific agencies and professional institutions wherever practical.
