# Child Safety Foundation

Orish's World is intended for birth–16 with experiences changing significantly by age.

- Ages 0–2 are parent/guardian-led; no independent AI chat design.
- Talking AI must be age-adaptive and bounded by a child-safety policy layer.
- Parent back-channel wording is private and should be transformed into positive child-facing activities rather than quoted back to the child.
- No unrestricted stranger chat, voice rooms or private messages.
- Any future social play requires parent-approved access and preset safe communication where appropriate.
- Food guidance must not shame, force or punish eating.
- Behaviour learning should use stories, missions, role-play and repair rather than humiliation or coercion.
- Evidence capture must be transparent and permission-controlled.
- Safeguarding escalation design must be reviewed by qualified professionals before production use.

## V1.3 routines, missions and cooking
- Parent-created missions are transformed before reaching child mode; parent wording is never shown as “your parent says…”.
- Routine completion has no score, streak penalty or punishment mechanic. Encouragement focuses on effort, sequencing and growing independence.
- Breakfast/food steps may be part of a family routine, but eating is never used as a behavioural reward or punishment.
- Kitchen Lab only presents recipes that match the adult-entered ingredients/equipment in the current starter library.
- Heat, knives and other clearly hazardous cooking steps are designated GROWN-UP TURN.
- Allergy and food-safety checks remain an adult responsibility; the app is not a medical or allergy-management system.

## V1.4 reward safety
Explorer Stars are learning/progress encouragement only. They are not money, cannot be purchased, cannot be exchanged for real goods, and are not used to rank children publicly. The same activity earns stars only once per day to reduce grinding pressure. Routines and behaviour are not given character grades, punishment scores or food-linked rewards.


## V1.5 offline making and creative safety
- Make With Orish is designed to send children away from the screen to build, test and create with an appropriate adult level of supervision.
- Every maker project contains an age-specific safety note. For 0–2, the adult controls paper/materials and the activity is shared rather than independent.
- Small loose pieces, cords, high drop points, heavy bridge loads and throwing toward people/animals are explicitly avoided or adult-controlled.
- The Transforming Origami Star is framed as geometry/model-making, not as a weapon or projectile.
- Creative Studio does not require uploading child photos, handwriting, drawings, voice or personal story content.
- Evidence records completion and learning objectives rather than capturing the child's physical work.
- Visual sorting uses a tap alternative so drag interaction is not the only way to play.

## V1.6 Kitchen Lab safety boundary
- The child-facing app never treats a timer as proof that food is cooked or safe; final doneness/temperature is always a grown-up decision.
- Oven, microwave, hot-pan, hot-container, sharp-knife and similar steps remain GROWN-UP TURN.
- For ages 0–4, child-tagged kitchen actions are presented as TOGETHER rather than independent turns.
- Allergy/food-safety notes remain parent-controlled and should not contain diagnoses or detailed medical records in this prototype.
- No-scales measurements are approximate practical conversions; the supervising adult remains responsible for checking the recipe and real-world result.


## V1.7 Family Clubhouse safety boundary
- Family Clubhouse is a **closed family-together activity area**, not a social network. There are no public child profiles, stranger discovery/matching, private child messaging, follower systems or location sharing.
- The family-role selector asks only for a role (Parent/Guardian, Sibling, Grandparent or Other Approved Family); no family member name is needed.
- Cooperative quizzes do not rank family members and have no timer, loser state or humiliation mechanic.
- Treasure hunts are restricted to adult-approved safe areas and explicitly avoid stairs, climbing, locked rooms, dangerous storage, medicines/chemicals, hot/sharp areas, unsupervised outdoor searching and hidden keys/phones.
- Family science challenges use low-risk household materials; teen investigations explicitly exclude flames, hazardous chemicals, mains-electricity experiments and ingestion experiments.
- Family cooking remains inside Kitchen Lab role boundaries: the presence of a family member never converts an adult-only heat/knife/food-safety step into a child step.
- Story/perspective activities can use fictional scenarios; children are never required to disclose private family conflict or personal experiences.
- Completing a family activity stores only a compact shared learning result when enabled — not the family conversation, recording, names or individual scores.


## V1.8 accessibility and memory rules
- Accessibility options do not ask a child to disclose a diagnosis, disability or medical information.
- Display accommodations must not be framed as rewards, penalties or evidence of ability.
- Memory Lab has no countdown, speed leaderboard or loser state.
- Cards must remain operable with keyboard/tap controls and use readable text labels, not colour-only cues.
- 0–2 Memory Lab is explicitly shared with an adult rather than presented as an independent baby performance task.


## V1.9 Good News Beacon boundary
Good News Beacon uses static, manually written demo cards. It does not fetch live headlines or publish breaking news directly to children. A future live source must pass adult/editorial review, age-appropriateness checks, frightening-content filtering, source verification and a publication gate before child display. Beacon reading is not turned into a score, streak or behaviour record.

## V1.9 Parent Learning Summary boundary
The adult summary may aggregate compact Learning Passport records and local reward totals. It must not silently mine child conversations, Parent Studio private wording, family discussion, child artwork, voice, photos or location. The summary is not a safeguarding judgement, behaviour grade, diagnosis or formal attainment decision.


## V1.10 Observation Lab boundary
- Observation Lab uses only built-in fictional visual scenes.
- It does not request camera, photo-library, microphone, facial-recognition, biometric or location permission.
- Children are never asked to photograph their home, bedroom, school, family or surroundings to complete an observation activity.
- There is no countdown, public ranking or loser state.
- Younger modes are shared/adult-supported; older modes progressively teach the difference between direct observation and inference.
- Learning Passport evidence records the learning objective/completion only, not screenshots or recordings of what the child was viewing.

## V1.12 Logic Lab boundary
Logic Lab is deliberately untimed and has no leaderboard or public score. The youngest mode is parent-led. Sequencing activities use built-in content only and do not collect camera, microphone, location, contacts or family-chat data. Routine/cooking examples are learning scenarios rather than instructions to act without appropriate adult supervision.


## V1.12 literacy and keyboard safety
- Under-2 use remains adult-led; no independent baby keyboard task is introduced.
- Typing activities have no speed race, public rank or penalty for slower input.
- Practice words/sentences typed by a child are transient UI state and are not copied into Learning Passport exports.
- The engine does not request contact details, names, school names, location or other personal information as writing prompts.

## V1.14 branching-story safety

- Interactive choices are not used to label a child as good/bad, compliant/non-compliant, safe/unsafe, truthful/untruthful, or to infer personality.
- A less-helpful choice can lead to a repair path; the app does not shame or punish the child for exploring it.
- Exact story routes and choice labels are not saved to the Learning Passport.
- 0–2 remains parent-led shared interaction, not an independent chatbot experience.
- Older science stories separate observation, inference, confidence and uncertainty instead of rewarding dramatic claims.


## V1.14 Maths Lab safety
- No maths speed race, public ranking or failure label.
- Hints and retries are normal and not penalised as behaviour.
- 0–2 maths remains adult-led shared play.
- Raw working and answer text are not stored in Learning Passport evidence.


## V1.15 mystery, research and life-skills safeguards
- Mysteries & Unexplained content must distinguish **fact, claim, hypothesis, inference and unknown**; sensational or paranormal explanations must never be presented as established fact.
- The Bermuda Triangle and similar topics are curiosity hooks for evidence reasoning, not fear content. Younger age bands receive gentler map/weather/navigation versions.
- AI Evidence Detective does not give children unrestricted live web access in this prototype. It teaches how to formulate queries, check sources, compare evidence and verify citations.
- Raw research notebook text and drawing-canvas content are transient by default and are not automatically persisted or uploaded.
- Financial literacy uses pretend scenarios; there is no child purchasing flow, real-money balance, gambling mechanic, paid reward or investment solicitation.
- Legal/civics missions teach general reasoning only. They must state that jurisdiction matters and must not present personalised legal advice.
- Honey-containing recipes are hidden from the 0–2 profile because that band includes infants under 12 months and the app intentionally does not collect exact birth date.

## V1.16 Orish Intelligence Layer
- Orish can now choose among approved learning engines from a child request, active age band and optional local interests.
- The router cannot unlock Parent Studio, expose PINs/passwords, disable safety controls or execute generated code.
- 0–2 remains parent-led; independent text input stays disabled.
- Clearly harmful or explicit requests are not gamified. The local prototype asks the child to involve a trusted grown-up where appropriate.
- Child prompt text is transient and is not automatically written to the Learning Passport.
- Private parent back-channel wording is not exposed to or quoted in child chat.


## V1.17 parent-control enforcement
- Parent/guardian can disable free-text Ask Orish for a profile; it is always disabled for 0–2.
- Parent/guardian can disable Family Clubhouse, Kitchen Lab, Good News Beacon and parent-created Mission HQ content.
- Family Clubhouse only presents role categories explicitly approved by the adult; names, stranger discovery and public invitations are not added.
- Spoken support can be disabled centrally and remains non-recording.
- Learning Passport evidence can be disabled centrally and does not store free-form chat, typed practice text, story route details, family discussion or research-notebook raw content.
- Live AI, open web search, public social features, location and child camera/uploads remain unavailable in this prototype.

## V1.18 connected-world navigation
The district map is a presentation/navigation layer over the existing reviewed activity engines. Filtering a district never unlocks disabled content. Parent Studio and the Parent Gate are deliberately excluded from the child quick dock. Featured journey artwork opens existing age-adaptive engines and does not create new public/social pathways.


## V1.19 release-readiness safety boundary
Parent Studio local unlocks expire after 15 minutes of inactivity. Installation/offline status does not weaken Parent Controls. The PWA cache contains application shell files only and no child conversation, raw voice, location, camera media or Learning Passport data. Real child deployment still requires production identity/access controls, privacy/safeguarding review and physical-device QA.

## V1.22 My Avatar Lab
The child-facing avatar creator uses pre-approved appearance parts and palettes only. Children can represent themselves or choose fantasy colours without the app inferring protected characteristics from those choices. The prototype does not request selfies, live camera access, face scans or unrestricted image-to-3D generation. Future Character Forge output must be reviewed before assets enter the child catalogue, and third-party generation interfaces must not be exposed directly to children.


## V1.23 3D avatar safety boundary

The child customises only approved local mesh groups and palettes. The child cannot upload an arbitrary model, run image-to-3D generation, import external URLs, or execute generated code. Realistic and fantasy skin colours are both allowed as self-expression options. The local GLB renderer falls back to the earlier safe renderer when device 3D support is unavailable.


## V1.24 living-avatar boundary
Avatar movement is limited to approved local pose actions (idle, wave, power pose and celebrate). Children cannot upload motion files, execute arbitrary animation code or connect the child avatar to an unrestricted external avatar service. The in-world explorer preview is generated from the local model, never from a child camera feed.


## V1.25 history and culture safety
History/culture learning must not stereotype a child, infer identity from appearance, or require disclosure of ethnicity, religion, ancestry, migration status or family history. Black history is available year-round rather than being framed as a seasonal novelty. Living cultural practices must be presented with named community/place/time context and variation; the app should not label one practice as representing every member of a culture.


## V1.26 — Are We Alone?

Added the age-adaptive, source-backed extraterrestrial-life evidence investigation. See `ARE-WE-ALONE.md`. Children do not get unrestricted web access; claims are never upgraded to facts without verifiable evidence.

## V1.27 voice controls

- Two-way microphone is OFF by default and must be enabled per child profile by a grown-up.
- Ages 0–2 remain parent-led and the child microphone option is forced OFF.
- No background/always-listening mode is used.
- A child must tap to start a voice turn; turns auto-stop after 12 seconds.
- The app closes microphone tracks at the end of the turn.
- Raw voice is not intentionally kept in browser storage, Learning Passport or exports.
- Voice transcripts are transient in this prototype and are not used for behaviour grading or diagnosis.
- A future production service must enforce the same policy server-side; browser controls alone are not sufficient security.
